Fable 5 Is Here — and the Price Tag Is the Real Story

If you're budgeting for frontier AI, Fable 5 lists at double Opus 4.8's price — so "use the best model everywhere" just got expensive. Reserve it for the work that genuinely needs it.

The honest version of today's Anthropic launch is two stories, and only one of them is the one you'll see in the headlines.

The headline is Claude Fable 5, generally available as of today across the Claude API, Bedrock, Vertex AI and Microsoft Foundry. Anthropic is calling it its most capable widely released model — built for demanding reasoning and the kind of long-horizon agentic work where a model has to stay coherent across hours, not turns. It carries a one-million-token context window and up to 128k tokens of output, and it reasons by default: adaptive thinking is always on, with no toggle to switch it off. That last detail matters more than it sounds.

The second story is the price. Fable 5 runs $10 per million input tokens and $50 per million output tokens. For comparison, Opus 4.8 — Anthropic's top Opus-tier model, released barely two weeks ago — sits at $5 and $25. Fable is exactly double. So the real decision today isn't "is it better." Of course the newest frontier model is better. The decision is whether the work in front of you is worth twice the bill.

I've watched enough rollouts to know what happens next. A team wires the shiniest model into everything — the summarizer, the classifier, the chatbot that answers "what are your hours" — and the invoice arrives a month later looking like a mortgage payment. Most enterprise AI work is not frontier work. It's retrieval, extraction, routing, and drafting, and a mid-tier model handles it without blinking. Fable 5 earns its keep on the genuinely hard problems: multi-step agents that plan and act, deep code work across a large repository, analysis that has to hold a thousand moving parts in its head at once. Point it there. Don't point it at your FAQ.

The always-on reasoning is the part to test before you commit. A model that always thinks is more capable, but you give up a lever — you can't dial the thinking down when a user is waiting. For interactive, customer-facing flows, benchmark the response times yourself before you assume it's a drop-in upgrade.

The quieter release is Claude Mythos 5, and it's not for most of us. It's limited-availability through something Anthropic calls Project Glasswing, positioned as a successor to its earlier invitation-only preview and aimed at defensive cybersecurity. You don't sign up; you get invited through your account team. Worth knowing it exists — file it under "ask about it if your security team is serious about AI-assisted defense," not "evaluate this quarter."

So here's where I land. Fable 5 is a real step up, and the cost is real too — and pretending the second part doesn't exist is how pilots quietly bankrupt themselves. Treat it like a scalpel, not a default.

Your move this week: pull your current model spend and tag each workload "frontier" or "routine." Route only the frontier work to Fable 5; leave the rest where it is — so you learn the upgrade's true cost before it surprises you.
